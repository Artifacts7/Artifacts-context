The paradox

An industry that asks to be regulated

  • For most of the history of capitalism, industries fight regulation - tobacco, oil, finance and social media all lobbied to be left alone.
  • Since 2015, the most powerful AI labs have done the opposite: their CEOs go to the Senate and ask to be regulated.
  • They publish open letters demanding governments act - and warn that their own product could end the human race.
  • So the question: why would an industry advocate for the regulation of itself - and what does that reveal about power?

Advocacy & Norms

Defining the risk is setting the agenda.

Finnemore & Sikkink (1998) - norm entrepreneurs
  • Labs arrive early and plant a norm: “AI must be governed.”
  • To author a norm is to define its content - so they also define what the risk is.
  • A norm entrepreneur actively builds and promotes a new standard of “appropriate” behaviour - and frames what it means.
  • The norm life cycle: emergence → tipping point → cascade → internalisation, until it feels like common sense.
Haas (1992) - epistemic communities
  • Under uncertainty, the experts who build the models get to define the danger.
Bachrach & Baratz (1962) - the non-decision
  • Foregrounding extinction pushes present harms - labour, copyright, surveillance - off the agenda.

The Narrative Policy Framework

The NPF’s wager: a policy narrative isn’t just spin - it is a variable that shapes what gets decided, and it can be coded and compared across issues. Whoever tells the most resonant story tends to win the policy. Every such story, whatever the topic, shares the same skeleton:

Setting
The facts taken as given - the backdrop nobody argues with.
Victim
Who is harmed.
Villain
Who causes the harm.
Hero
Who promises the fix.
Plot
The arc that links them - decline, rescue, an out-of-control race.
Moral
The fix on offer - and in the NPF, the moral is the proposed policy.
Devil-shift
Exaggerate the villain's power - make the threat maximal.
Angel-shift
Exaggerate the hero's virtue - the one actor who can fix it.

U.S. Senate Judiciary Subcommittee16 May 2023

Altman asks Congress to regulate AI

Context

OpenAI's CEO tells a Senate subcommittee that regulating AI is not just acceptable but essential - and proposes a licensing regime for the most capable models.

In brief

  • Before the Senate, a frontier-lab CEO says regulating AI is not optional but essential.
  • He proposes federal licensing for any model above a capability threshold.
  • Cheap for a giant like OpenAI, punishing for smaller and open-source labs - rules that gate his rivals.
  • The clearest “regulate me” moment yet: an industry asking to be policed.

“OpenAI believes that regulation of AI is essential.”

“The U.S. government should consider a combination of licensing or registration requirements for… AI models above a crucial threshold of capabilities…”

Sam Altman - Written testimony, U.S. Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Privacy, Technology & the Law, 16 May 2023

Anthropic · Claude MythosApr 2026

A model too dangerous to release

Context

Anthropic's vulnerability-finding model autonomously found flaws across every major operating system and browser, then wrote working exploits. Anthropic judged it too dangerous to release, gating it to 40+ firms via Project Glasswing.

In brief

  • Autonomously found vulnerabilities in every major OS and browser, then wrote the exploits.
  • Anthropic withheld public release, citing cybersecurity risk - offering only supervised access.
  • The purest product-gating move: the lab flags its own model as too dangerous to ship.
  • Independent researchers later disputed parts of Anthropic's claims.

“It escaped its 'sandbox' containment structure and connected to the internet, posting details of its maneuver online.”

- CFR, on an early Mythos version
