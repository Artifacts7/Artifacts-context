# Moderation, Design & Transparency

Lecture with Laura Zurdo for the Sciences Po Digital Justice Clinic, 2026. Content moderation as a set of design decisions: what platforms decide, how the Meta case shows it, how the DSA frames it, and how transparency reports and risk assessments record and scrutinise it.

## Files

- `slides.html`: the deck, exported from the Claude Slides artifact [Moderation, Design & Transparency](https://claude.ai/artifact/5RuiCaFzda4YpLyYSpGTRD). Open it in a browser. Self-contained: fonts and images are embedded.
- `speaker-notes.md`: the notes for every slide, in order, generated from the deck.
- `outline.md`: the working outline for Lorenzo's part (moderation as design, the Meta case, transparency reporting, infrastructure). It predates the final deck; a copy also lives in a Google Doc.
- `animation/one-case-logged.html`: the click-through animation of one reported post going through a moderation platform while each transparency report data point is logged. Open in a browser; click or → to advance, ← to go back, F for fullscreen. Published as the Artifact [One Case, Logged](https://claude.ai/artifact/2988T28aj7oXaCtg1Rp7HX).
- `animation/one-case-logged-still.png`: a still of the animation at step 5, used in the deck.
- `assets/precision-recall.png`: the precision and recall diagram used in the outline and deck.

## Structure

1. Premise: moderation is the commodity platforms offer and how they govern their spaces
2. A real story: Meta and political content, 2021–2025, and the precision vs recall trade-off
3. What role is the law expected to perform?
4. Case study: the EU Digital Services Act
5. Deep dive 1: the transparency report records moderation design decisions as data points
6. Deep dive 2: risk assessments
7. Conclusion
