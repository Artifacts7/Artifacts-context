# Shut that door
**Subtitle:** And what we should really get from age verification
**Date:** Dec 13, 2025
**URL:** https://artifactstech.substack.com/p/shut-that-door

---

Australia's eSafety Commissioner has implemented regulations blocking anyone under 16 from accessing major social platforms like Snapchat, YouTube, Facebook, and Instagram. Platforms must implement age verification systems using selfies, ID documents, or payment cards, facing fines up to A$50 million for non-compliance.

This regulatory movement extends globally. Twenty-five U.S. states have passed age verification laws with federal regulation under discussion. The UK's Online Safety Act restricts minors from platforms with adult content. The EU is exploring minimum age requirements of 16 for social media access.

## The Technology Challenge

Age verification involves distinguishing between "verification" (precise age identification) and "estimation" (threshold determination). Current methods—biometric analysis, document verification, behavioral patterns—each carry tradeoffs.

Technical obstacles include:
- Failing to recognize people with disabilities or minorities
- Excluding those without formal ID documentation
- Questionable data storage practices
- Emerging workarounds like VPN usage and parental photo substitution

## Accountability Questions

Critical gaps remain: Who implements checks—individual platforms, app stores, or devices? Who bears responsibility when minors circumvent systems—platforms, verification services, or parents?

## The Deeper Issue

Age verification addresses symptoms rather than causes. The real problem involves platform design itself. Algorithms optimized for engagement create inherently risky environments for all users, not just minors.

A meaningful solution requires examining why digital harms exist: they stem from deliberate design choices prioritizing engagement metrics over user welfare. Rather than merely restricting access, platforms should shift toward designs emphasizing user satisfaction, transparency, and genuine control.

## Constructive Alternatives

Effective reform would involve:
- Measuring user satisfaction rather than engagement time
- Enabling researcher transparency into algorithmic operations
- Increasing user agency in content consumption
- Building literacy through informed participation

"Algorithms can serve people instead of people serving algorithms." Real safety emerges from architectural redesign, not gatekeeping alone.
