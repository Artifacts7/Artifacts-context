# Writing is thinking?
**Subtitle:** And what is the impact of LLMs on our ability to translate thoughts in words
**Date:** Feb 10, 2026
**URL:** https://artifactstech.substack.com/p/writing-is-thinking

---

We've all experienced it: drafting work emails with AI assistance has become commonplace. But the broader implications are concerning.

We now live in an era where generating substantial volumes of language is faster and easier than ever. An LLM can produce ten pages of coherent content in minutes. Yet this efficiency masks a troubling reality: **volume doesn't equal meaning**.

LLMs excel at producing text that sounds polished and organizes information neatly—whether compressed into bullet points or expanded into lengthy documents. However, as one observer noted, these systems represent "technologies of average" in both writing quality and intellectual substance.

The problem extends beyond creation. Reading has similarly been simplified. We increasingly ask AI to summarize documents that someone else asked AI to lengthen. Smartphones and news apps now automatically extract key points, creating what might be called "the accordion of AI language"—text endlessly compressed or expanded based on convenience rather than necessity.

## The Core Concern: Language as Commodity

The troubling shift is that producing language has become commodified. This efficiency allows us to bypass the difficult, essential work of translating thought into words.

What we're witnessing is "meta-writing": instructing machines what to write and how, then delegating the actual composition. This creates a fundamental problem—**we think less to produce language**. Writing once compelled structured, intentional thought. Now it's becoming a back-and-forth negotiation with algorithms.

## Lost Skills

This shift erodes critical capacities:

- **Close reading of our own work**: We assume machines will catch errors, so we become less careful first readers
- **Deliberate emphasis**: Without laboring through drafts, we lose awareness of what deserves prominence
- **Clarity through constraint**: The ability to express complex ideas simply—a hallmark of deep understanding—atrophies when we can instantly request rewrites
- **Self-discovery**: Writing traditionally revealed gaps in our own thinking. That revelatory process disappears when composition becomes frictionless

## The Human Element

Reading and writing aren't merely about information transfer. They're acts of connection. We engage with others' writing not just for content—which machines can provide—but to understand what moved them, how they think, what they value.

A text resonates because it carries the marks of a human thinking process. That authorial presence, that genuine struggle to communicate, cannot be replicated by systems trained on statistical patterns.
