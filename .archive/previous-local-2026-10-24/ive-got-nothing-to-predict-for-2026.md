**Subtitle:** But I've a few questions and would love to hear yours!
**Date:** Dec 23, 2025
**URL:** https://artifactstech.substack.com/p/ive-got-nothing-to-predict-for-2026

Rather than adding another prediction list, the author poses open questions about emerging tech trends for 2026.

## Key Questions for 2026

**Compensation for Content in AI Training**
Currently, no standardized payment exists when AI systems scrape Wikipedia or news outlets. Wikipedia reports that "65% of its most expensive traffic comes from bots," meaning the platform bears costs while receiving no compensation.

**AI Writing Quality**
Can we move away from recognizable AI writing patterns—"this, not that" constructions, awkward short sentences, and repeated phrases like "delve into"—and return to better prose standards?

**Purpose of AI Development**
AI was promoted as solving humanity's critical challenges, yet adoption priorities seem misaligned: erotic chatbots, parasocial companions, and endless content generation.

**Interface Design with AI**
As AI handles more computational work, interfaces simplify with fewer visible controls. This raises concerns about user agency and potential dark patterns operating behind the scenes.

**Age Verification on Social Platforms**
Australia and the UK have initiated age verification requirements; Europe and the US are considering similar policies. Will this become standard practice?

**Social Media Transparency**
Despite regulatory efforts and data access improvements, understanding how social platforms actually operate remains challenging.

**User Control Over Content**
Some users want greater ability to direct what appears in their feeds. Can this clash with retention-focused business models?

**Digital Detox as Social Currency**
Physical devices that reduce phone use and school bans on phones are gaining traction. Might avoiding social media become culturally valued?

**Competition and Innovation**
Antitrust efforts and the EU's Digital Markets Act may create space for new companies and innovation if momentum continues.

**AI in Politics**
Will an "Obama moment" for AI occur—when a political candidate's pervasive AI use in messaging and targeting makes risks obvious to everyone?

**LLM Limitations**
Yann LeCun and others question whether large language models suffice. World model AIs designed to understand cause-and-effect may offer alternatives to hallucination-prone systems.

**AI Market Viability**
Will the AI bubble eventually burst, and what implications might follow?
