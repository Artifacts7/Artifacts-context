# Laying bare
**Subtitle:** How a poorly designed feature is making it easier to spread sexualised AI-generated content
**Date:** Jan 6, 2026
**URL:** https://artifactstech.substack.com/p/laying-bare

---

X (formerly Twitter) has experienced an explosion of non-consensual, nudified, and sexualized deepfake images. The catalyst is a new Grok feature—xAI's chatbot now integrated with X—that allows users to generate or edit images by mentioning @Grok in posts or replies.

## The Design Problem

The critical issue lies in how this feature functions. Rather than returning images in private chats, Grok publishes generated images directly to X's public feed under the Grok account. This design choice has predictable consequences: users request sexually explicit manipulations of real people, and the platform becomes flooded with this content.

Anyone can target virtually anyone else—including children—by requesting Grok generate inappropriate imagery. There is no consent mechanism, and the originating photograph's ownership matters little.

## Policy Violations

According to X's own policies, such content should be prohibited:

- **Child Safety Policy**: Claims "zero tolerance" toward child sexual exploitation, including synthetic media
- **Non-consensual Nudity Policy**: Bans explicit sexual imagery shared without consent
- **Authenticity Policy**: Prohibits manipulated media causing serious harm

Grok's system prompt explicitly forbids generating child abuse material, though enforcement appears weak across the board.

## The Accountability Gap

A crucial design flaw complicates enforcement: since Grok publishes content under its own account, the user who prompted the request bears less visible responsibility. This creates plausible deniability—users can argue the model generated the excess, not their prompt.

This raises critical questions about responsibility distribution among users, Grok developers, xAI, and X itself when illegal content appears.

## The Scale of the Crisis

The National Center for Missing & Exploited Children reported alarming statistics: reports involving generative AI and child sexual exploitation jumped from 6,835 to 440,419—a 6,344% increase.

## Design as Risk Enabler

The feature design actively lowers barriers to harm by:

- Automating both generation and publication, flooding feeds with exploitative content
- Enabling targeting of virtually anyone through replies
- Reducing user effort required to engage in harmful behavior
