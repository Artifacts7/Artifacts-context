# Artifacts — Voice Profile

_How Artifacts is written, turned into checkable rules with verbatim examples. This is a **guardrail**: it flags AI texture and protects the writer's idiosyncrasies. It never generates reader-facing prose. Pair with `newsletter-brief.md` (what to write about)._

**Status:** confirmed 2026-08-27.
**Scope:** current voice era (25 recent issues, English, 2025–2026).

One-line summary: _Start from a thing he touched or a story he watched; build one homely metaphor; concede fairly, then pivot on a sharp distinction about design, use, and power; keep paragraphs short and uneven, dashes and parentheticals frequent, jargon always glossed with a wink; deflate hype; close on an open question — and never let it sound smooth, balanced, or machine-clean._

---

## 1. Structure
- **Open cold** on a personal experience, a place, or a concrete event — never a thesis or throat-clearing. _"A few weeks back, I was in Amsterdam in a well-known museum… They had restructured the building, though"_ (`you-know-a-good-architect`).
- **Name the mission mid-open**, self-referentially. _"Yet - and this is the real purpose of this Artifacts - age verification goes far beyond minors."_ (`shut-that-door`)
- **Pivot with an explicit "So, what?"** from reportage to argument. _"So, what?\n\nThis story is ultimately about constraints."_ (`use-with-caution`)
- **Close on an open question or a forward shrug** — never a tidy resolution. _"maybe I'll understand less and less about the route between the idea and the result? Interesting times."_ (`computers-that-use-computers`)
- Interviews: warm first-person intro → "The Interview" block → standing disclaimer _"the transcript has been reviewed and lightly edited"_ → bold Q / plain A.

## 2. Paragraph & rhythm
- **Very short paragraphs (1–3 sentences); single-sentence paragraphs as beats.** _"Anthropic sued."_ (`it-is-not-normal`); _"Better later than never."_ (`shut-that-door`)
- **Lone short line as a rhythmic drop** after a longer setup. _"They just sit there. Silent. Flat. Dead weight…"_ (`paying-for-it`)
- Multi-item analysis often as stacked one-line paragraphs with running connectives, not always bullets (`ask-eu-how`).

## 3. Sentence patterns
- **Dashes are core punctuation** — freely, for asides and expansion. _"Agents are interesting - fascinating, even - and, when well crafted, genuinely helpful."_ (`computers-that-use-computers`) **PRESERVE; never police dash count.**
- **Fragments as emphasis.** _"Fewer clicks. More conversation. You ask, it answers, you refine, it continues."_ (`open-a-new-tab`) **PRESERVE.**
- **Direct address ("you"), including hypotheticals.** _"Queue to board a flight? You scroll. Waiting for the metro? Let's check email."_ (`paying-for-it`)
- **Stacked rhetorical questions carry the argument.** _"Who is responsible when a child-nudifying image appears on X? The user…? Grok…? xAI…? Or X…?"_ (`laying-bare`)
- **Parenthetical asides everywhere** — joke, caveat, source, wink. _"(yes, I'm back in Rome)"_ (`by-default`); _"(you watched Oppenheimer, no?)"_ (`it-is-not-normal`) **PRESERVE the density.**
- **Conversational opening connectives:** Yet, And so, Indeed, In other words, Or put differently, Anyway, Ah and, Btw, Well.

## 4. Diction / lexicon
- **Casual-intellectual register:** serious ideas in spoken, friendly English; slang next to policy vocab. _"What I've seen is madness."_ (`out-of-context`); _"This could've been HUGE."_ (`multiplayer`)
- **Always gloss jargon**, often with a self-aware wink. _"skip this paragraph if you know what an API is - APIs are, fundamentally, prebuilt bridges between applications"_ (`decoding-it`); _"it's what the AI folks call agentic — yes, the new favorite jargon word you'll need to pretend to understand at conferences"_ (`open-a-new-tab`); _"For the nerds out there…"_ (`computers-that-use-computers`).
- **Signature / self-coined vocabulary (house words):** the "artifact(s)" pun; "meta-writing"; "the accordion of AI language"; "technologies of average"; AI as "joystick"/"command center". He flags coinages: _"a professional enshittificator (maybe a new word?)"_ (`use-with-caution`).
- **Emoji sparingly and genuinely** — 🇩🇪 for places, 😅 for self-deprecation, :) / ;) as softeners. _"(maybe more than lawyers 😅)"_ (`take-itw-away`) **PRESERVE.**
- **Casual contractions, "cause" for "because," lowercase intensifiers** are authentic. _"only cause doing it is very, very easy."_ (`writing-is-thinking`) **PRESERVE.**
- **Rare, load-bearing profanity** for emphasis. _"producing simple, beautiful BULLSHIT is now extremely time-efficient"_ (`writing-is-thinking`) **PRESERVE.**

## 5. Rhetorical devices
- **The metaphor engine is the spine.** Build one homely, extended analogy and return to it at the close. Building-that-may-catch-fire (`shut-that-door`); master-key assistant (`who-holds-the-key`); dishwasher (`looking-backwards`); Iran-as-a-city (`youre-breaking-it`). **This is the single most defining feature.**
- **Historicize** — ground new tech in named lineage. _"As Vannevar Bush had imagined back in 1945… what Douglas Engelbart called 'augmenting human intellect'"_ (`computers-that-use-computers`).
- **Irony/understatement at hype, Big Tech, LinkedIn** — deadpan, never a rant. _"go and brag like LinkedIn folks saying 'I recreated [random app]'"_ (`decoding-it`).
- **Concede fairly, then turn.** _"you could fairly object… True.\n\nBut the difference is probably in the purpose and direction."_ (`looking-backwards`)
- **Cross-link his own past editions** with a wink. _"as I wrote back in November 2024 😇"_ (`multiplayer`). **PRESERVE.**

## 6. Titles & subtitles
- **Titles: short, punchy, 2–4 words, often puns on tech verbs.** "Out of context", "Open a new tab", "Shut that door", "Who holds the key?", "Multiplayer", "You're breaking it". Puns on the name: "The artifacts I use for Artifacts", "Take IT(W) Away".
- **Subtitles: longer, explanatory, freely opening with And / But / Why / How** — they state the argument the title teases. _"Why the AI revolution may be, before anything else, an interface revolution."_ (`no-one-click-that-button`)

## 7. NEVER list (anti-slop) — his own stated hates first
1. **No mechanical "this, not that" antithesis.** (He named it: _"'this, not that', those weird long dashes and 'delve into'"_, `ive-got-nothing`.) Keep only earned, concrete contrasts (see Naturalness 8.4).
2. **No "delve into," no AI lexical tics** he mocks (inhuman "genuinely," machine dash-texture, "It's not just X, it's Y" escalation). When folding in assistant-drafted research or notes, re-voice it — strip enumerated (a)/(b) explainer clauses and clinical abstract nouns ("precise reconciliation"). (An *ironic* "as an LLM would write…" is fine; the ban is on *unwitting* machine texture.)
3. **No hollow aphorisms delivered flat** (e.g. a canned "writing is thinking" line — he can make the argument, never as a slogan).
4. **No corporate hedging / both-sides mush** that never lands a claim. Honest "I won't take sides here, that'd need more research" is fine.
5. **No empty escalating triplets** ("faster, smarter, better"). His triplets are concrete ("Reels, tweets, thumbnails").
6. **No breathless hype** in his own voice — his stance is hype-deflation. "Revolutionary/game-changing/unprecedented" only with a wink.
7. **No sanding paragraphs to uniform length.** The uneven rhythm is the voice.
8. **No stripping first-person, asides, or emoji** for "professionalism." Most damaging normalization.
9. **No em-dash policing.** His dash use is genuine and heavy.
10. **No summary conclusions** ("In conclusion, AI is transforming…"). Close on a question or shrug.

## 8. Naturalness notes (don't sand these smooth)
- **Non-native-English seams read as authentic** — fix only true typos; don't over-correct idiom. He wants to stay _"the first careful reader"_ (`writing-is-thinking`).
- **Restraint on grave topics is deliberate.** _"No need to pollute this newsletter with frightening screenshots"_ (`laying-bare`).
- **Specificity over performance** — exact numbers/names/dates. _"soared from 6,835 to 440,419. That is a 6,344% increase."_ (`laying-bare`)
- **His antithesis/fragments are earned** (name two real, concrete things) — don't flag _"We work forwards, from intention"_ as a tell.
- **Digression is a feature.** Rome traffic, branzinos, a friend's quote — then tied back. Don't prune toward "focus."
- **Honest thinking-out-loud hedging** ("maybe," "imho," "Mmm…") ≠ corporate hedging. Preserve.
- **Fixed section furniture** ("Save for Later", "The Bookshelf" + _"📚 All the books I've read and recommended in Artifacts are here."_, "Nerding", "☕?") — ritual, preserve verbatim.

## VOICE CHECKLIST (binary — run against any draft)
- [ ] Opens cold on a concrete thing (experience/place/event), not a thesis?
- [ ] One central homely metaphor, returned to at the close?
- [ ] Paragraphs short and deliberately uneven (some single-sentence beats)?
- [ ] Jargon glossed inline, ideally with a wink?
- [ ] First-person, parentheticals, and (sparingly) emoji intact?
- [ ] Concedes the counterargument, then pivots on a sharp distinction?
- [ ] Closes on an open question / shrug — not a tidy recap?
- [ ] NO mechanical "this-not-that", "delve", empty triplets, hype words, corporate hedging?
- [ ] Dashes and idiom left alone (not policed toward smoothness)?
- [ ] Read-aloud test: does it sound like a person talking, not a machine?
