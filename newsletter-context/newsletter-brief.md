## The thesis

The design of technology is never neutral. Every button, default, algorithm, ranking, or restriction is the residue of a decision — made by a platform, a government, a regulator, or a market. Artifacts reads those decisions through the interfaces we touch every day: it treats a product, a feature change, or a rule as *a choice with an author*, and asks who it serves, who's accountable, and what it quietly forecloses.

The name is the argument. It comes from Langdon Winner's 1980 "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" — the claim that technological objects carry political properties, establish power dynamics, and, in effect, do politics. So the newsletter's job is not to review technology but to *read* it: to find the decision hiding inside the design, and to follow it out to its social, political, and epistemic consequences.

Crucially, Artifacts also enacts its own thesis. Its recurring intellectual claim — that the value is in the *process*, the route from A to B, not the polished output — is the same principle behind how it's made. The form is the argument.

## What it's about — and how each topic is treated

- **Interfaces & the shift to natural-language / agentic computing** (the largest strand). Not "cool new AI tools," but: interfaces are shrinking or disappearing, and that reshapes who holds power over how we reach information. Treated through concrete artifacts — Google AI Mode collapsing the ten blue links, AI browsers, the chatbot-as-command-center, agents as "computer–computer interaction." And, increasingly, agents as the web's *readers* — access rules (robots.txt, paywalls, licensing) and the web reshaping for non-human consumers (llms.txt, the Markdown revival).
- **Platform design, harm, and accountability.** Design as the site of harm and the site of liability — product-negligence verdicts, §230, nudify features read as *product decisions*, age verification as "design governance." The move is always from the interface to the accountability question.
- **AI as a strategic, non-neutral technology, and the state.** AI as "not a normal technology"; the "design of use" layered on top of the system (Anthropic vs. the Pentagon); internet shutdowns and the splinternet; European sovereignty and the Eurostack.
- **Writing, thinking, creativity — process vs. output.** AI as a machine that "looks backwards, from probability" while creativity "works forwards, from intention"; "meta-writing"; building an editorial AI stack that protects rather than replaces the thinking.
- **Open AI, data, and power over information.** Open models and concentration risk; data access as a counterweight to platform black boxes; who owns the "fuel."
- **Attention and the analog counterweight.** The phone as a default; the book as an interface that doesn't reply back.
- **The European tech ecosystem**, mostly via event dispatches and interviews — the "Brussels bubble" translated for outsiders.

Politics is core — but a specific politics: **power, governance, accountability, who-decides**. Not party/electoral politics, and not geopolitics-as-current-events. Artifacts approaches even state-level stories sideways, through design.

## The patterns (analytical moves that recur)

- **Interface change → power decision.** The visible artifact is never cosmetic; it's where a decision becomes legible.
- **Separate the "design of use" from the design of the system** — a distinct governance layer sitting on top of the technology.
- **Process over output.** Prefer the route, the intention, the "how we got here" to the polished result.
- **Historicize to judge the present.** Read today against 1996, against Engelbart and Vannevar Bush, against the Gutenberg parenthesis.
- **Follow the accountability / the money when AI intermediates.** "Who's responsible when…?" "Who gets paid when ChatGPT books the hotel?" And who's accountable when your *agent* acts — when it breaks a site's rules on your behalf (the human, the lab, or the agent)?
- **Deflate hype without doom.** AI is "not magic, not hype" — but not the end of the world either. Anti-booster *and* anti-Luddite.
- **Reframe rather than adjudicate** — "the *real* purpose of this Artifacts is…" — and **close on an open question**, not a verdict. This open-ended, debate-holding close is a deliberate, protected signature.

## Signature vocabulary

The "artifact(s)" pun as the unit of analysis (apps, books, institutions, even his own AI agents — "a small team of artifacts that help me write Artifacts"). Self-coined terms: "the design of use," "design governance," "meta-writing," "the accordion of AI language," "technologies of average," "computer–computer interaction." The metaphor engine: every abstract claim earns a concrete analogy (the unsafe platform as a building that may catch fire; "call the architects, not the artists"; the AI assistant with a master key; Iran as a city closing its bridges; "you don't need a Ferrari to go to the supermarket").

## Objectives & vision

**Is (today):** a translational bridge — reading the design of technology for European, tech-literate-but-not-engineer readers near policy, product, and media. Its distinctive value is the "in between": explaining mechanism to smart non-specialists, and naming the "fundamental disconnection between those designing regulation and those building the technology."

**Becoming:** the *same altitude, wider resonance*. Keep translating for smart non-engineers — but make each piece genuinely interesting to technical and builder readers too. Interesting *to* them, not written *only* for them. Deeper mechanism where it earns its keep, without abandoning the bridge that is the newsletter's edge. Comfortable with "some politics" — power and governance stay central, in measure, still no party lines.

## Boundaries & audience

**Audience:** European vantage, global scope. Tech-literate knowledge workers near policy/product/media; increasingly, technical readers who want the power/design angle they don't get elsewhere. Civic framing — "not just consumers, but citizens."

**Boundaries:** not a news newsletter (events are pretexts for a design argument); no party/electoral politics, no geopolitics-as-events; refuses to lawyer-up or adjudicate; no predictions; won't outsource the core creative act (topic, argument, writing stay the author's); won't reproduce harmful imagery; anti-hype and anti-doom.

## Open tensions (live, worth watching)

- **Widen without diluting.** "Interesting to tech people too" pulls toward more mechanism; the bridge altitude pulls toward translation. The edge is holding both — watch for drift into either pure explainer or insider-baseball.
- **Vendor-neutral vs. Anthropic-as-protagonist.** Claude/Anthropic recurs far more than any other company (antagonist-of-the-Pentagon, tool-of-production, Save-for-Later regular). Worth being deliberate about.
- **Open questions vs. payoff.** The protected open-ended close is a strength; the risk is a reader occasionally wanting the stance you're withholding. Hold the signature, but know when a piece has earned a verdict.
