VERBATIM EXCERPTS FROM THE OP-ED

On the need for regulation (via PBS NewsHour / AP, "Google CEO calls for regulation of artificial intelligence"):

"There is no question in my mind that artificial intelligence needs to be regulated. The question is how best to approach this."

"Sensible regulation must also take a proportionate approach, balancing potential harms with social opportunities."

Opening / personal framing (via SiliconRepublic, "Sundar Pichai on regulating AI: 'Technology's virtues aren't guaranteed'"):

"Growing up in India, I was fascinated by technology. Each new invention changed my family's life in meaningful ways. The telephone saved us long trips to the hospital for test results."

"The refrigerator meant we could spend less time preparing meals, and television allowed us to see the world news and cricket matches we had only imagined while listening to the short-wave radio."

"Yet history is full of examples of how technology's virtues aren't guaranteed."

"Internal combustion engines allowed people to travel beyond their own areas but also caused more accidents."

"The internet made it possible to connect with anyone and get information from anywhere, but also easier for misinformation to spread."

"Companies such as ours cannot just build promising new technology and let market forces decide how it will be used."

"International alignment will be critical to making global standards work."

"AI has the potential to improve billions of lives, and the biggest risk may be failing to do so."

---

Additional context reported by secondary coverage (paraphrase, not verbatim): Pichai pointed to the perils of nefarious uses of facial recognition and of deepfakes, argued regulation should balance mitigating potential harms with preserving social opportunities, and suggested existing frameworks such as the EU's GDPR could serve as a foundation for AI rules, with international alignment resting on agreement over core values.
