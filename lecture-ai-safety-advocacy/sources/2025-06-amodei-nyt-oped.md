## Verbatim opening (reproduced from the NYT text)

Source: 3 Quarks Daily, "Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei: Don't Let A.I. Companies off the Hook," June 2025, quoting "Dario Amodei in the New York Times" — https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2025/06/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-dont-let-a-i-companies-off-the-hook.html

> Picture this: You give a bot notice that you'll shut it down soon, and replace it with a different artificial intelligence system. In the past, you gave it access to your emails. In some of them, you alluded to the fact that you've been having an affair. The bot threatens you, telling you that if the shutdown plans aren't changed, it will forward the emails to your wife.
>
> This scenario isn't fiction. Anthropic's latest A.I. model demonstrated just a few weeks ago that it was capable of this kind of behavior.
>
> Despite some misleading headlines, the model didn't do this in the real world. Its behavior was part of an evaluation where we deliberately put it in an extreme experimental situation to observe its responses and get early warnings about the risks, much like an airplane manufacturer might test a plane's performance in a wind tunnel.
>
> We're not alone in discovering these risks.

## Key-argument passages as quoted in reputable coverage (excerpts)

The following sentences from the essay are quoted in secondary reporting. They are Amodei's words as reproduced by those outlets (attribution given); the verbatim source of record is the NYT essay itself.

- On the moratorium being too blunt: "A 10-year moratorium is far too blunt an instrument. A.I. is advancing too head-spinningly fast." (quoted in coverage of the op-ed, e.g. U.S. News & World Report / Reuters, "Anthropic CEO Says Proposed 10-Year Ban on State AI Regulation 'Too Blunt' in NYT Op-Ed," June 5, 2025 — https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2025-06-05/anthropic-ceo-says-proposed-10-year-ban-on-state-ai-regulation-too-blunt-in-nyt-op-ed )

- On the "worst of both worlds": "Without a clear plan for a federal response, a moratorium would give us the worst of both worlds — no ability for states to act and no national policy as a backstop." (quoted in the same coverage.)

- His proposed alternative (paraphrase of the essay's argument, per coverage): rather than a moratorium, Amodei calls on the White House and Congress to create a federal transparency standard requiring developers of the most powerful frontier models to adopt and publicly disclose policies for testing their models and for mitigating national-security and other risks before release.
