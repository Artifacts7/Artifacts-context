# AI Safety Advocacy — Lecture Timeline

Working timeline of pivotal events in how AI labs (and adjacent voices) have advocated for "AI safety." Read for rhetoric and dynamics: mission-framing → product-gating → call-for-regulation → shape-the-regulation → dissent.

---

## 2014 — Bostrom's *Superintelligence*
Nick Bostrom publishes *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies*. The x-risk canon — it gives the existential-risk framing its intellectual scaffolding and vocabulary, which lab CEOs later draw on directly.

## December 2015 — OpenAI founded
Launched as a nonprofit "to ensure that artificial general intelligence benefits all of humanity." Safety-as-mission is the founding rhetoric: the lab is itself an advocacy position before it ships anything.

## July 2017 — Musk: regulate AI before it's a danger
Elon Musk tells US governors AI needs *proactive* regulation. Already invested in OpenAI and other AI firms; had spent three years calling AI "more dangerous than nukes" and "summoning the demon." Signed the Jan 2015 FLI open letter with Hawking.

## March 2018 — Zuckerberg "not sure we shouldn't be regulated"
Post-Cambridge Analytica. Mostly about data, but gestures at AI: as AI tools improve, what is companies' legal responsibility to catch offending content?

## December 2018 — Microsoft alarms over facial recognition
Brad Smith (Brookings): governments should start regulating facial recognition in 2019; calls for a "floor of responsibility" against a race to the bottom. Opens the door to voluntary regulation.

## January 2020 — Pichai: "no question" AI needs regulation
Alphabet CEO in an FT editorial: "Companies such as ours cannot just build promising new technology and let market forces decide how it will be used." Framed around facial-recognition fears.

## November 2022 — ChatGPT launches *(bridge, not an advocacy beat)*
Not a safety-advocacy moment, but the engine of everything after it. Generative AI goes from research curiosity to a product in millions of hands almost overnight. This is why the dormant "we must govern AI" norm (2014–2020) suddenly detonates into the May 2023 crescendo — the abstraction became a thing people could touch.

## March 2023 — "Pause giant AI experiments" open letter
FLI letter (signed by Musk and others) calls for a 6-month pause; if not, governments should impose a moratorium. Two weeks later Musk launches his own AI company.

## May 16, 2023 — Altman asks Congress to regulate AI
Senate hearing: Altman backs a new agency to regulate AI. "Benefits... vastly outweigh the risks," but "regulatory intervention by governments will be critical." (May 25: Anthropic's Amodei warns senators about AI-enabled bioweapons.)

## May 25, 2023 — Microsoft's "five ways to govern AI"
Brad Smith details a policy blueprint, e.g. mandated "safety brakes" in high-risk systems with government-verified testing. Industry shaping future regulation.

## May 30, 2023 — 22-word extinction statement
"Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside... pandemics and nuclear war." Signed by Altman, Hassabis, and others.

## September 2023 — Zuckerberg: "work together" on regulation
Closed Senate meeting; CEOs reportedly agree the industry needs regulation. Meta: "Congress should engage with AI to support innovation and safeguards."

### July 2023 – May 2024 — Flurry of nonbinding agreements
- Jul 2023: Meta, Google, OpenAI promise the White House to develop AI responsibly
- Sep 2023: More companies join the White House AI safety accord
- Nov 2023: UK AI Summit — developers and governments agree on testing
- May 2024: AI Seoul Summit — network of safety institutes

## August–September 2024 — SB 1047 fight
Musk backs California's SB 1047; Anthropic opposes (July), then supports (Sept); OpenAI opposes (Aug). Newsom vetoes (Sept), calling it not "the best approach."

## October 2024 — Anthropic: "targeted regulation"
Open letter: "Governments should urgently take action on AI policy in the next eighteen months. The window for proactive risk prevention is closing fast."

## January 2025 — OpenAI's "policy proposals"
Economic Blueprint opens by warning regulation stunted the UK auto industry; proposes "democratic AI" grounded in the country's democratic values.

## June 2025 — Amodei: don't let AI companies off the hook
NYT guest essay against the proposed 10-year moratorium on state AI regulation. Argues not for a slowdown but a "national transparency standard."

## September 2025 — Anthropic endorses SB 53
Early support for California's transparency bill (safety reporting + whistleblower protection); it becomes law. OpenAI lobbied against it.

## September 2026 — Industry "agrees to slow down"
Rogue AI agents attack companies; OpenAI says it hit the brakes (Aug); a senior Anthropic safety researcher resigns in protest (Sept 8), citing a 10% chance AI kills all humans by decade's end. Trump calls AI fears "a sick conspiracy."
