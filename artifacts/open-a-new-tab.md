Searching on Google will soon be <strong>like this:</strong>

<a href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!usGS!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F39a20ed5-87a7-4b15-8e46-32089b5a07d1_432x432.gif"><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!usGS!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F39a20ed5-87a7-4b15-8e46-32089b5a07d1_432x432.gif" alt=""></a>

Not just because the <a href="https://www.zdnet.com/article/googles-im-feeling-lucky-button-might-soon-be-replaced-by-ai-mode/">“I’m feeling lucky” button is disappearing</a>, but because <strong>Google has officially introduced <a href="https://blog.google/products/search/ai-mode-search/">something it calls “AI Mode.”</a></strong>

This means that instead of just tossing you a list of blue links, <strong>Google Search will now host a conversational AI assistant.</strong> Ask it questions, follow up with more, get crisp, synthesized responses (with small numbers referencing websites)

<a href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Uc5z!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe7dcc66c-b226-4ecf-a084-b813c962f6e8_1920x1080.png"><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Uc5z!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe7dcc66c-b226-4ecf-a084-b813c962f6e8_1920x1080.png" alt=""></a>

Think of it less like searching the web and more like… <strong>chatting with it.</strong>

(And, well, they also rolled out a bunch of other stuff nicely wrapped up in a <a href="https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/953b658a-579b-4b3c-b280-43b3781babf3">mind map</a> by - of course - NotebookLM, one of Google’s own AI tools.)

Now, from a technological standpoint, <strong>this isn’t exactly groundbreaking anymore.</strong> On Artifacts, we’ve been talking about AI-driven search <a href="https://open.substack.com/pub/artifactstech/p/ho-parlato-con-internet?r=2d3svm&amp;utm_campaign=post&amp;utm_medium=web&amp;showWelcomeOnShare=false">since February 2024</a>. By now, <strong>many of us are already familiar with the likes of ChatGPT or Perplexity</strong> digging through the web on our behalf.

In short: <strong>we’re used to talking to the internet.</strong>

<strong>The shift already happened</strong> - from dry, scattered link lists to tight, tailored responses. But the lingering question has been: <em><strong>what is Google waiting for?</strong></em> Now we know.

And commercially? <strong>Well, this might just be the opening scene of a new era.</strong> Here’s why:

- <strong>Google</strong>, that for most of us equals the Internet, <strong>is embracing and shaping what they called <a href="https://www.theverge.com/2024/5/14/24155321/google-search-ai-results-page-gemini-overview">“a total re-imagination of search”.</a></strong> It is, in a sense, the Internet going in a whole new direction.

- This is not just a tool enabling AI search. <strong>It’s Google baking AI into everything.</strong> Search, yes, but also Gmail, Docs, Drive. The whole productivity suite is being rewired with intelligent, conversational search assistance.

And unlike Perplexity or other AI tools, <strong>Google’s shift will be impossible to ignore.</strong> It’ll be right there, front and center, for the billions who already default to it. No tutorials needed. No effort required. Just… different.

<strong>And search is just likely to be radically something else in a couple of years.</strong>

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Here’s how it works, <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/26/what-is-google-gemini-ai/">according to the people building it:</a>

> <em>When you ask a complex question, Google’s AI-powered Search kicks into “advanced reasoning” mode. It uses its custom Gemini model to break your query into smaller parts and fires off multiple searches at once.</em>
>
> <em>It digs deep across the web and taps into Google’s real-time data sources like the Knowledge Graph, Shopping Graph, and even local info from Google Maps contributors.</em>
>
> <em>Then it assembles a polished answer. All of that, from just one query - and fast.</em>

As the Verge put it, it’s <strong><a href="https://www.theverge.com/google/671200/google-googling-ai-mode-project-mariner-i-o-2025">Google doing a lot of Googling.</a></strong>

But <strong>this is bigger than search.</strong>

First off, it’s what the AI folks call <strong>agentic</strong> — yes, the new favorite jargon word you’ll need to pretend to understand at conferences. But jargon aside, the idea is simple: <strong>it doesn’t just find things</strong> <em><strong>for</strong></em> <strong>you, it does the finding, the reading, the summarizing</strong> — and hands you back the insight.

Which brings us to the bigger shift: <strong>the interface itself.</strong>

<strong>Fewer clicks. More conversation.</strong> You ask, it answers, you refine, it continues. A back-and-forth, not a click-and-hope.

<strong>It’s a new User Experience</strong> — one that’s less about hunting through links and more about getting <em>usable</em> content, right away.

<img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HH8x!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0760492e-5168-4904-8eef-28e6296a73bf_1440x608.png" alt="">

Indeed, people are no longer <a href="https://www.bain.com/insights/goodbye-clicks-hello-ai-zero-click-search-redefines-marketing/#">clicking</a> (+ an <a href="https://chatgpt.com/share/683768fd-5bd0-800a-8bad-2e988256df2d">explanation</a> of the chart as it’s not the clearest thing lol)

As Google puts it:

> <em>The way we’ve all Googled for two decades was largely a response to the structure of the web itself: web pages in, web pages out. <strong>Good AI models are now able to get around that structure, and find and synthesize information from lots of sources</strong></em>

Put plainly: <strong>it’s just easier.</strong> And probably irreversible.

<a href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WcTd!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F14ef9519-7fa7-441d-9ad1-e6125b417c9d_1024x1024.png"><img src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WcTd!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F14ef9519-7fa7-441d-9ad1-e6125b417c9d_1024x1024.png" alt=""></a>

if search has to be a back and forth with AI, ping pong is the best case of back and forth for sure

All of this will, of course, <strong>not come without implications.</strong>

<strong>First casualty: the humble web page.</strong> As we’ve explored before, AI search means machines read pages, rewrite the content, and feed it back to users — <strong>without users actually visiting the source.</strong> Less traffic. Less visibility. Less ad revenue.

Websites and especially media are not happy about this. They’ve called this <a href="https://www.newsmediaalliance.org/google-ai-mode-statement/">“a definition of theft”.</a> In response, Pichai (CEO at Google) said <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1IxG7ywSNXk">they expect AI to direct even more traffic to websites.</a>

<strong>Second problem: Google’s own business model.</strong> If no one’s clicking on pages, then traditional ads - <strong>the ones that made Google \$198 billion last year</strong> - lose their audience. So now Google is playing both sides: disrupting the old model while still trying to milk it.

<strong>It’s the innovator’s dilemma:</strong> evolve or protect what’s already working. In this case, Google needs web content to train and feed its AI, and publishers need Google to reach audiences. A delicate co-dependence.

<strong>Finally, the SEO apocalypse.</strong> For two decades, websites played the game of rising to the top of Google’s results. Now, the real prize is to be included in the AI-generated answer. Buried in a citation.

That’s a whole new game - <strong>not of visibility, but of</strong> <em><strong>inclusion</strong></em><strong>.</strong> Across Google, OpenAI, Perplexity, whoever ends up writing the answers the internet reads.

What does this mean for media? Business? Education? Any site that once relied on search-driven traffic?

<strong>That’s the billion-click question.</strong>

We’ve gone from a layered, intricate Internet - Google, links, websites, ads - to <strong>a flattened experience where AI intermediates it all.</strong>

<strong>The number of interfaces is shrinking.</strong> The AI now browses for us.

Sure, <strong>talking to the internet is easier than clicking through it.</strong>

But what that means for who owns attention - and who profits from it - is still very much up for grabs.
