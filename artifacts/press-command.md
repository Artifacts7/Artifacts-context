# Press Command
**Subtitle:** Why chatbots are going beyond being platforms, despite what many say
**Date:** Oct 22, 2025
**URL:** https://artifactstech.substack.com/p/press-command

---

Many have claimed that AI companies are replicating Apple's iPhone strategy from 2008—turning their product into a platform where users access apps. This comparison holds merit, though it's incomplete and somewhat myopic.

We're witnessing something more expansive: **chatbots represent a new internet usage layer**, not merely platforms.

## The Current Limitations

The author has previously argued that chatbots often underperform: users must start from scratch, paste content and images, and constantly supply context. AI-powered browsers represent an improvement, functioning as "a joystick for the Internet" by embedding AI directly in user workflows.

## Recent Developments

**OpenAI's Apps for ChatGPT** enable users to:
- Book accommodations via Booking
- Create personalized Spotify playlists
- Design presentations using Canva

Anthropic launched "Skills," allowing marketing material generation without repetition. Their Model Context Protocol (MCP) establishes standards for chatbot-app integration.

## Beyond Platform Theory

While the "platform" designation fits economically—matching the OECD's definition of services facilitating interdependent user interactions—the reality extends further.

Rather than replacing existing platforms, chatbots concentrate access into a unified chat interface. This represents a **new Internet layer** that:

- Preserves existing platforms while mediating access
- Simplifies interaction through natural language
- Hides complexity behind conversational interfaces
- Treats the AI model as the product itself

The distinction matters: interfaces shrink toward text boxes, where intelligence translates natural language into platform actions.

## Emerging Complications

**Economic tensions surface immediately**: Who compensates services like Booking when ChatGPT handles transactions? How will OpenAI prioritize platform access?

More fundamentally, this new operating system depends entirely on external infrastructure. The moment Booking or similar services restrict access, the system loses functionality. Chatbots don't operate independently—they're ultimately reliant on others' APIs, accounts, and services.

## Future Questions

As chatbots become the primary interface for platform access, the critical question emerges: **Will services redesign for AI interaction rather than human button-clicking?**
